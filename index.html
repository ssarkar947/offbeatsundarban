How It Begins

The Journey Starts at the Jetty Ghat

The trip starts early in the morning. After pickup from Canning and a drive to Dhamakhali, you arrive at the riverside ghat where the river stretches out broad and calm under the morning mist.

You step onto the wooden boat, settle in, and are handed a piping hot cup of ginger-cardamom tea in an earthen clay cup (bharer cha). The crew loads fresh vegetables and river fish bought from the morning market, unties the mooring ropes, and the boat slowly heads into the current.

From this moment on, city sounds and phone notifications fade away. As you glide along the water, your boatmen point out kingfishers perched on mangrove branches, scan the mudbanks for crocodile basking spots and fresh tiger pugmarks, and tell you the old stories of Ma Bonbibi that every delta family knows by heart.

“In the Sundarbans, you don’t rush. The forest opens up to you when you slow down, follow the tides, and listen to the river.” — Subrata Mondal, Senior Local Guide

Near Bangladesh Border

Burirdabri Watch Tower & Mudwalk

Overlooking the Raimangal River & Bangladesh

Burirdabri is one of the most memorable stops in the entire forest. It has an enclosed wooden boardwalk that lets you safely walk right over the mudflats. Below your feet, thousands of pencil-like breathing roots (pneumatophores) poke out of the soil, while fiddler crabs and mudskippers scurry around. From the top of the tower, you get a panoramic view straight across the river into the Bangladesh Sundarbans.

  • The Cage Mudwalk: Walk safely over the muddy forest floor without getting dirty or disturbing the wildlife.
  • View of Bangladesh: Look across the broad Raimangal river to see the continuous forest on the other side.
  • Bird Watching: Great spot for kingfishers, egrets, and sea eagles circling overhead.

Easternmost Watchtower

Jhingakhali Watch Tower

Eastern Buffer Zone

Located right at the eastern fringe of the Indian Sundarbans, Jhingakhali sees only a tiny fraction of tourist boats. There is a sweetwater pond in front of the watchtower where spotted deer, wild boars, and birds come to drink, especially in the afternoon heat. The boat ride through the narrow tree-lined creeks getting here is peaceful and relaxing.

  • Sweetwater Pond: Sit quietly on the tower and watch deer and wild boars come out of the forest to drink.
  • Quiet Creeks: Dense trees line both sides of the water where you rarely hear another engine.
  • Local Village Market: We visit the nearby local village market on Day 3 morning to see daily delta life.

The People Behind the Forest

Stories of Bonbibi and Life in the Delta

The Sundarbans is not just trees and animals. It is home to millions of people who have lived beside the tidal waters for generations, respecting the forest and its unwritten laws.

Why Every Boatman Bows to Bonbibi

Before any fisherman, crab catcher, or honey collector sets foot in a creek, they pause and offer a prayer to Ma Bonbibi, the forest guardian deity revered equally by Hindus and Muslims alike. The belief is simple: take only what you need to feed your family, show respect, and the jungle will let you return home safely.

  • The Mowalis (Honey Gatherers): Every spring, brave local villagers venture into the dense tiger forest to gather wild mangrove honey.
  • Evening Tales: After a day on the river, our crew sits with you to share real folklore, tiger encounters, and delta history.
  • Bhatiali Songs: Listen to traditional river songs sung by the boatmen under the quiet evening sky.

Elusive Sovereign

The famous swimming tiger of Bengal. Even when they stay hidden, you will often spot their fresh pugmarks pressed into the morning mud.

Frequently Spotted

Saltwater Crocodile

Crocodylus porosus

The largest reptile on earth. During winter low tides, they love to climb out of the water and lie motionless on the sun-warmed mudflats.

Pitta megarhyncha

A colorful, jewel-like bird that hops along the muddy forest floor searching for small crabs under the mangrove roots.

Clear answers to questions travelers often ask us.

How do we reach the starting point?

Our trip starts with a pickup from Canning railway station (easily reached by local train from Sealdah, Kolkata). From Canning, we drive you in an auto or car to Dhamakhali ghat where our boat is waiting. If you prefer a direct car from your home in Kolkata, we can arrange an AC car transfer as an add-on.

What is included in the ₹3,500 package?

The ₹3,500 per person rate includes all 3 days of boat cruising, 2 nights clean hotel accommodation in the delta, 6 major meals plus tea and snacks cooked fresh on the boat, all Forest Department entry tokens, and a dedicated local guide and boat crew. There are no surprise extra fees.

Is the trip safe for senior citizens and young kids?

Yes, completely safe. The boat has stable seating with covered shade, life jackets for everyone, and our crew helps elderly guests step carefully at the boarding points. All watchtowers (like Burirdabri and Jhingakhali) are fully fenced with heavy wire mesh per Forest Department safety standards.

Can you provide vegetarian or Jain food?

Yes, absolutely. Just let us know in advance when booking. We cook pure vegetarian food (dal, seasonal delta vegetables, paneer, aloo bhaja) in separate clean pans, and can prepare food without onion and garlic for Jain guests.
